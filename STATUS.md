- **The Mechanism (the bar), rebuilt from the renewed design** (`pleamar helix bar/helix-v2-mooi/`, ported in mooi's tokens; D13, slimmed to v4 in D14, exposed in D15, **turned on its side to v6 in D16**): one machine along a **72 px strip** across the top of the screen, and nothing else — no panel, no rail, no rack, no backing of any kind. The workspace positions *are* the bar: each is a **triangular prism turning on the bar's own axis**, 46 px of bar wide on a 52 px pitch, tied to its neighbour by a **link** through the gap and four **rods** carrying each of its vertices to the nearest two of the next block's, so the positions read as one machined chain rather than a row of cards. On the same axis, to the right of the chain: the player's reading, its **transport** (a plate two pitches wide, hairline-divided into back · play or pause · on, struck through when there is nothing to drive), the **clock**, the **System door** (which carries the battery on its second face), the four readings (**volume, brightness, Bluetooth, network**) and the bar's three ways out of itself (the focused window's actions, the **Atlas**, the **search**) — then the faceted **cap** with the bar's own error dot. To the left of the chain sits the bar's identity — **the mark**: a frame holding a miniature spine and one accent detent, which opens the bar's menu — joined to the chain's barrel by a **neck**; a second neck carries the barrel across into the row, so the bar has a beginning and an end and nothing in between (the rotary dial that used to stand there is **gone**, D17). A hairline **rule** divides the chain from the row, and every reading carries the same link-and-collar joint as the chain, so all of it is one machine. Nothing is painted behind any of it: the desktop shows through the strip wherever there is no prism, rod, link or collar, and the strip's own empty space passes clicks through to the window underneath (pleamar commits the input region from what is actually painted, so the strip's own empty space takes no click either — measured on the running desktop). Two rules still hold it together: *geometry carries state, colour only confirms* (`accent.primary` is what is, `focus.ring` is what is pointed at), and *the compositor is the only authority* — a press or a notch asks `workspaces.focus` at once, and the chain moves when the *service* reports the change, so the block at the focus slot is always where you really are (with the dial gone, nothing on the bar can point anywhere else). The surface reserves the 72 px strip alone, so the bar never floats over a window, and what opens under it (the mark's menu) takes no click (pleamar commits the input region from what is painted).
  - **The chain slides along its axis, and turns about it — and it is solid.** The block the compositor is on always arrives at a focus slot inside the chain's own window. Block *i*, `d = i − pos` from the slot, slides by `d · g.tick` (52 px a step) and is turned by `36° · d + 180° · roll` plus a parity term, so the working block comes round face-on while its neighbours are turned a little in front of and a little behind it. A face is drawn only while it faces you and **narrows in height as it turns away** — the prism's three faces are 120° apart and their edges slide up and down the block — so a block reads as a solid turning in space rather than a flat mark; its chamfered faces, its outline, its four bolts, its label and its half of the link are all derived from that one angle and that one position, and **nothing about it is transparent**: measured per column on the running desktop, a block is 46–54 px of graphite across its whole width with the wallpaper only above and below it. What decides how much of the chain is seen is the chain's own **window**: everything it draws is clipped to its zone (3.75 pitches either side of the slot, so seven of the ten positions at most), a block entering or leaving view is *cut off* by the window's edge, and only blocks inside it take a press — the fade that used to draw the outermost block at 12 % of its opacity (a ghost, and what “the blocks look transparent” meant) is gone, and so are `g.reach` and `g.fade`. The **barrel** runs the whole width of the window at the blocks' own height with a **joint disc** in every gap, so the chain is one mechanism even where the list has nothing — workspace 1 has no position to its left, which is where a bare window used to look like a broken machine. `pos` and `roll` follow `active_row` alone, on separate springs (`m.chain` 300 ms, `m.roll` 450 ms — measured at 99 % of travel **233 ms** and **267 ms**, with no overshoot past the position asked for: peaks 1.9999 and 1.9936 of a two-step move), and `roll` does arrive after `pos`, so a change slides and then rolls. Asked for a second workspace 200 ms into the first change, the chain takes **333 ms** to reach the new one and never passes it: the springs converge on the last thing asked for, which is what makes an interrupted change land cleanly.
  - **The readings turn over.** A press turns a prism to its next face (`m.turn` 260 ms — measured **233 ms** at 99 % of travel; the dial that used to share that clock is gone): what each of the three faces shows is written by the scene once per face, so a reading is a glyph, then a number, then a word. The readings and the clock are `Module`s; the **System door** and the bar's three ways out of itself are `Door`s — the same prism, held still, whose press opens a surface (a component cannot take `event = none`, so the duplication is the honest shape of it, D13 §12). The transport drives the player through `media_cmd` and is drawn from the `Media` facts, the wheel over the **volume** module sets the level through the same `vol` event the System island uses, and a block's label is the position's own: `01 … 10` for a workspace with no name, a named one's name shortened to three characters (`lib/spaces.luau` owns that rule — Hyprland calls an unnamed workspace after its number, so a name that *is* the number counts as no name at all).
  - What D12 promised still holds: network (signal bars, a link mark for a cable, a struck line when offline), Bluetooth (the rune, lit while the radio is on, a dot while it scans) and brightness (a sun sized by the level) still carry their state in their geometry; a module the machine cannot have is not drawn and the row closes the gap. Bluetooth is shown and never toggled: there is no documented write for it, so a switch that cannot be trusted to reflect would be worse than a state that can. Written up in docs/MECHANISM.md, decided in docs/DECISIONS.md D13, D16, D17 and D18.
  - **v7 — the shaft, the dark, and the colours (D18).** Three things the screen asked for. **The ends of the chain were gaps:** workspace 1 has nothing to its left and workspace 10 nothing to its right, and with the barrel painted only *inside* the window those states showed a dark hole between the chain and the section beside it. There is now one **steel shaft** under the whole of Region B — from the mark's own frame to the rule before the row — and the window is a **sleeve** over it: the blocks are threaded on the shaft, a joint disc sits on it in every gap, and where the chain has run out what is seen is the steel it turns on. It is shaded as a cylinder (five bands, the top third in light, a specular hairline, a darker foot) and closed by a collar at each end. **Matt black and neon:** the body is the palette's darkest surface (`bg.primary`) laid in eight bands under a grain of hairlines clipped to the slab, the pieces sit a step up from it, and the marks are the only light — every face of a prism is shaded by the angle it is turned to (`bg.secondary` → `surface.elevated`), and the mark on the lit block is drawn twice, a wide soft stroke under a sharp one, so it reads as neon on matt black. **Smaller with it:** the strip reserves 56 px (was 64), the machine is **769 px** wide (was 874, 1112 before that), a block is 38 px on a 42 px pitch, the window 2.5 pitches either side of the slot. **Colour per mark:** `bar.tones` gives any of the bar's thirteen marks a colour of its own (`chain`, `media`, `transport`, `clock`, `system`, `volume`, `brightness`, `bluetooth`, `network`, `window`, `atlas`, `search`, `cap`), each defaulting to a palette **role** in `lib/theme.luau` (`M.TONES`) — so one wallpaper already gives the bar several colours — painted through the same three-facts-per-colour path the tokens use, and a name that is not a mark is reported while the rest still apply. **The wallpaper follows on its own:** `theme.source` defaults to `wallpaper` and `bin/mooi theme-watch` runs `theme-sync` whenever the wallpaper changes, started by `mooi start`/`takeover` and ended by `mooi stop`, with one sync at startup when the palette's recorded source (`palette.from`) is not the wallpaper now.

## Known gaps

- Multi-monitor untested (the LG was not connected). The Spine and current Workspace Atlas surface use one monitor.
- Islands cannot be dismissed by clicking elsewhere (a layer surface does not hear outside clicks). Esc, the Spine buttons, × and 30 s idle dismiss them.
- While an island holds the keyboard, Hyprland will not move the keyboard to a window: choosing a window (membrane ↵, its Focus satellite, an Atlas tile) closes the island first and dispatches once it has let go (~60 ms). If the focus does not happen, that island comes back with the reason.
- The Atlas board is rebuilt while it is open, so a window opening or closing moves the tiles under the selection (the selection is an index), and the move strip's subject follows it. A window that moves workspace while the strip is open is not re-read until the next report.
- The move picker is walked with ←→ or clicked: a scene key cannot be a digit (`key 1` is a load error), so a workspace cannot be typed at.
- Moving a window to the workspace it is already on is dispatched like any other move and reported as it comes back (it says it moved); it is not short-circuited.
- The theme's palette comes from Matugen's **dark** scheme (mooi is dark-first); a light scheme is not built, and neither is a named theme package or a Workshop UI for any of it. `theme.overrides` is validated but has no editor: it is a settings file today.
- The theme pipeline is proved from the palette file (a staged one and a real `theme-sync` run); the wallpaper hook is **mooi's own** now (`bin/mooi theme-watch`, started by `mooi start` — D18), so there is nothing for you to add; mooi still does not edit your config.
- **Nothing on the bar can preview a workspace any more.** The dial that could point at a workspace the compositor has not been asked for is gone (D17, deliberate): a press or a notch asks `workspaces.focus` and the chain follows the service. If a preview is ever wanted again it has to come back as a stated instrument, not as a needle.
- The helix shows seven of the ten positions at a time (`fl.reach` 3.5 either side of the focus slot); the Atlas lists them all at once.
- The compact Ferrolume bar needs about 1350 px with every module shown; on a narrower screen hide modules (bar menu → MODULES) — nothing collapses on its own yet. The System island still opens bottom-left (the old spine's place) and the islands keep the older theme rather than Ferrolume.
- Hover glows on the bar are written but not verified on screen: `hyprctl movecursor` does not send the surface hover events.
- `bin/mooi check`: 51 unit tests, 0 failed (the `lib/theme` fallback failure is fixed — D21).
- Global modes (Focus / Explore / …) are still only specified.
- Takeover has no notifications, tray or lock screen of its own yet (lock falls back to swaylock).

Found and fixed while rebuilding the bar from the renewed design (D13):

11. **The old chain check could not survive a sliding chain, and a sliding chain changes what the check must say.** The suite counted labelled block zones and wanted one per configured position; with the chain sliding, a position out of reach is not drawn and its zone is inactive, so `describe` (which lists what takes input) names only the window. The check now proves the three things that actually hold: the ids on the rail are a **contiguous run**, exactly **one** block is the active one, and that one is centred on the focus slot (read from `describe`'s own panel height) — a stronger check than the count it replaces.
12. **`event = none` is not a thing, so a module cannot be a door by parameter.** `component Module(…, press: event = none)` is a load error ("there is no event called 'none'"), and a copy's block may not declare rules either (it says the component needs a `children` to put them in). The bar therefore has two components, not one with a switch: `Module` (a prism that turns over) and `Door` (the same frame and one face, held still, whose press opens a surface). The duplication is the honest shape of it.
13. **A nested `pick` is not a text.** `text pick(a, "x", pick(b, "y", "z"))` is a load error; the flat form is what the language takes, so the network module's third face reads `online`/`offline` and the wired/wifi distinction stays on the glyph (the link mark) where geometry already carries it.
14. **Zone rectangles reach past what is painted, and that is the mechanism.** The volume module's wheel zone is declared *before* the module so its press stays on top — `describe` reports it as "covered by hit#Module", which for a press is exactly right and for the wheel is not (scroll reaches through).
