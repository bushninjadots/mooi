What decides how much of the chain is seen is the chain's own **window**: everything it draws is clipped to its zone (3.75 pitches either side of the slot, so seven of the ten positions at most), a block entering or leaving view is *cut off* by the window's edge, and only blocks inside it take a press — the fade that used to draw the outermost block at 12 % of its opacity (a ghost, and what “the blocks look transparent” meant) is gone, and so are `g.reach` and `g.fade`. The **barrel** runs the whole width of the window at the blocks' own height with a **joint disc** in every gap, so the chain is one mechanism even where the list has nothing — workspace 1 has no position to its left, which is where a bare window used to look like a broken machine. `pos` and `roll` follow `active_row` alone, on separate springs (`m.chain` 300 ms, `m.roll` 450 ms — measured at 99 % of travel **233 ms** and **267 ms**, with no overshoot past the position asked for: peaks 1.9999 and 1.9936 of a two-step move), and `roll` does arrive after `pos`, so a change slides and then rolls. Asked for a second workspace 200 ms into the first change, the chain takes **333 ms** to reach the new one and never passes it: the springs converge on the last thing asked for, which is what makes an interrupted change land cleanly.
  - **The readings turn over.** A press turns a prism to its next face (`m.turn` 260 ms — measured **233 ms** at 99 % of travel; the dial that used to share that clock is gone): what each of the three faces shows is written by the scene once per face, so a reading is a glyph, then a number, then a word.
